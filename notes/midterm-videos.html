1 Every concept you need

ConceptsnewOpen the MP4

One pass through the course, built on the professors' growth model. Read the cards below before you watch and again after: they are the points every answer is built from.

Remember these

  1. Growth = more workers + more output per workerReal GDP grows through the labour channel or the productivity channel. Only productivity keeps raising living standards.
  2. Productivity has three sourcesCapital deepening, labour quality (skills, education) and innovation. Institutions sit underneath all of them.
  3. Diminishing returns to capitalA water buffalo transforms a subsistence rice farm; one more tractor barely moves a Canadian wheat farm. So poor countries can grow fast by investing, but catch-up is conditional.
  4. Institutions are the root causeCapital and skills are proximate causes. Inclusive institutions (the two Nogales) allow creative destruction; extractive ones block it.
  5. Middle-income trap and the three i'sInvestment gets a country to middle income. Infusion (adopting foreign technology and practices) and then innovation get it out.
  6. Structural transformationWorkers leave low-productivity farms for industry and services, and inequality tends to rise then fall (Kuznets curve).
  7. Potential vs actual GDPPotential is what the economy can sustainably produce. Below it in a slump, above it in an unsustainable boom.
  8. Canada grew by adding peopleBusiness productivity fell from 83% of the US level in 2002 to 73% in 2019. Business investment is weak.
  9. New: degrees are not productivityCanada has the most degrees in the OECD (nearly two thirds of adults); Germany has 34.3% yet is over 30% more productive. Education needs capital, the right industries, adoption, competition and matched skills to pay off.
  10. Trilemma logicThree goals, only two at once. Say which two you pick and what you give up.
  11. Could appear: trade and ChinaGains from trade come mostly from imports: competition, scale, infused ideas, cheaper capital goods. China is strong on all three i's and slowing anyway.

2 How to approach the exam

ExamnewOpen the MP4

All three past papers follow the same shape, so you can walk in knowing what each question will ask. The ladder below is the shape; the rules under it are what the answer keys give marks for.

Every paper climbs the same four rungs

Rung 1Say what the exhibit shows
  • 2025: potential vs actual crop yields
  • 2024: the trilemma
  • Makeup Q1: define the puzzle
Rung 2Explain it with the growth model
  • Labour channel vs productivity channel
  • Name the concept: diminishing returns, structural transformation
Rung 3Apply it to a country
  • Usually Canada, with class numbers
  • Makeup Q2: three Canada hypotheses; Q3: Germany only
Rung 4Take a side on a policy
  • First words: "Yes, good idea" or "No, bad idea"
  • Either side scores if reasoned

Remember these

  1. Verdict in the first sentenceThe keys give marks for the answer itself before the reasoning. Never make the marker hunt for it.
  2. Name the course conceptThe keys literally ask for terms like "structural transformation" and "diminishing returns to capital". Use the course words.
  3. Use the exhibit's numbersQuote what the chart shows and cover both countries or both lines. A skipped given fact is a lost mark.
  4. Answer only what is asked"Define the puzzle" means do not explain the cause. "Focus on Germany" means no Canada.
  5. Make hypotheses truly differentCapital, industry mix and adoption are three ideas. Three flavours of "not enough investment" are one.
  6. Time by marksRead everything for 4 minutes, then give each question 76 × its marks / total. Makeup paper (50 marks): 15, 23, 15 and 23 minutes.
  7. Plan in bullets firstThirty seconds of verdict, concept, evidence beats rewriting. Stay under each word cap.
  8. If time runs outA verdict plus a named concept on every question beats one polished answer and a blank.
  9. No calculatorRound to easy numbers: 75 / 30 = 2.5; 70 / 2% = 35 years to double.
